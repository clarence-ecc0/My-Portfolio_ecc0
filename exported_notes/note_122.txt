Numbers 20&#58;10-12,7
Moses heard the instructions from the Lord but did another thing else- He disobeyed God's instructions, because of anger. He insulted the people of God (calling them rebels), he boasted as if he was the one that brought the water out of the rock, and he struck the rock, even twice. God interpreted his actions as unbelief and disrespect for Him. Anger caused this for him, and cost him entrance into the land of Canaan. Watch over your anger.

Point-1&#58; The serious consequences of the manifested anger of Moses
a. The cause and the cost of Moses' anger
Psalms 106&#58;32-33; Deuteronomy 4&#58;21-22; 1&#58;27
We must not allow people outside of us to provoke our spirit and cause us to respond, act, or talk inadvisedly. 

b. The coarseness and curse in ministerial anger; 
2 Samuel 12&#58;5-7; Numbers 22&#58; 27-33; 2 Chronicles 25&#58; 7-13
When the judgment that David, in his righteous anger had pronounced came back to hunt him in the sickness of his son (Beersheba's child), he went and prayed that the child should live, and not die as he would have caused to happen based on his  judgement. 
The Ass of Balaam acted in love, to save Balaam but was punished for it. Some of these may be happening in our homes, place of business, etc. we need to exercise restraints when people do things we are not happy with. 

c. The consequence and the condemnations of Messenger's anger.
Deuteronomy 3&#58;23-26
Moses who could pray for the whole tribe, for Miriam, for Aaron when they offended God and God would answer, he had his own challenge and God was not only angry with him but barred him from repeating his plea for mercy any further. That is tough.

Point-2&#58; The solemn consideration of meaningless anger in ministry 

a. The inconsiderate anger of thankless ministers
Jonah 4&#58;1-4; John 7&#58;23-24; Luke 15&#58;26-28; 
Jonah was angry that he preached to people (without even fasting & praying) and they repented. The question God asked was, are you sure you are right in this your anger? It is a question for all of us. Many angry people are inconsiderate and unreasonable.

b. The impetuous anger of thoughtless ministers 
Daniel 2&#58; 

Angry people are thoughtless and tempestuous. Daniel was 10 times better than all the wisemen in Babylon and when the query of Nebuchadnezzar could not be answered in the absence of Daniel, he did not think it wise to ask for Daniel but decreed all the wisemen (including Daniel that was not there) should be killed.

Point-3&#58; The steadfast conquerors of mortified anger in mastery 
2 Timothy 2&#58;5-7
God expects us to have mastery over anger. Anger is an innate nature of the fallen man and must be expunged from us as God's children. We are the first and the best to control ourselves.

a. Timeless demand of freedom from man's anger
Psalms 37&#58;8-10; Ephesians 4&#58;30-32; James 1&#58;19-20
Anger is not our best response but forgiveness. Forgiveness is the new nature of the redeemed man. Think of the repercussions of anger before you allow it to take control.
Our moment of anger (our display) can prevent someone from going to heaven, and can prevent us, ourselves from going to heaven. Control your moment of anger BECAUSE YOU ARE A CHILD OF GOD

b Thorough deliverance and freedom from anger
Romans 6&#58;6,18; Galatians 5&#58;19-21, 22;

c Total dominion through the fullness of the Messiah's atonement 
Galatians 2&#58;20; Philippians 2&#58;3-5
