What is grace? Grace means having a privilege that one is not entitled to, or which one has lost lawfully (by provision of existing law, e.g., of God)

What is salvation? To be delivered from the punishment that a committed offence prescribed for it. It means to be liberated from the prison of Satan where man was waiting until death before he starts his eternal punishment in hell.

Why do we need salvation? Man is a never dying soul.  He will live forever, but where? In God's presence (in His paradise as He had planned originally) forever if we are ready to accept and live by His word, His will and His way. The learning for this starts from here and the power to do it comes by grace. Alternatively, outside the presence of God (in hell that He has prepared for the devil and other disobedient created beings) who rejected His word, His will and His way. The decision for this starts from here and it is by rejecting His grace.
 Grace is not passive; grace is active.


1. The necessity for grace in securing our salvation 
Satan, that wicked one has no desire to release anyone that is ensnared through diverse lust into his prison of iniquity. He is like a spider that has spurned its web and is enticing souls into it. Isaiah captures the wickedness of Satan so accurately in Isaiah 14&#58;17. 

We were all lawful captives in that prison with no way out. We had no power to help ourselves as the apostle Paul cried out (Romans 7&#58;19-24,25). 
We were powerless until Christ came with grace, to grant unto us an unmerited favour, to open the prison door and set our souls free (Luke 4&#58;18,21; Isaiah 48&#58;24-25). Apostle Paul affirmed that we are saved through grace. Ephesians 2&#58;8

Grace is available through Christ, grace is free but grace has conditions to be met. The offer of grace is based upon a covenant, a better covenant with better promises as the Holy Ghost said in Hebrews 8&#58;6. What then is the covenant that grace brought us into? Salvation is available unto us only if we accept the word, the will and the way of God (deny ungodliness and worldly lust; live soberly, righteously and godly in this present world). Titus 2&#58;11-12


2. The reliance on grace in sustaining our salvation
Grace gives us power to resist the devil. The grace that admitted us through the strait gate of repentance (where we denied ungodliness and worldly lust) is still required to sustain us in the narrow way of sobriety, righteousness and godliness.

We may have been liberated from the prison of satan through grace but he has not given up. He desires to bring us back and fights back vehemently. 

Christ gave the command and the power to the disciples to go and preach and set the captives free. Philip obeyed and went to Samaria and Simon the sorcerer was dethroned and there was great liberation in the city. The Holy Ghost said, separate Paul and Barnabas unto me for the mission that I have for them (Acts 13&#58;1) and they moved and dethroned Bar Jesus (Elymas the sorcerer) (Acts 13&#58;4,6-12). Scriptures tells us that Satan fought back. Persecution started in Jerusalem and James was killed, and Peter was next; John Mark that accompanied Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13&#58;5) decided to separate from them (Acts 13&#58;13), setting up the program for the disbandment of the tag team of Paul and Barnabas in Acts 15&#58;37-38. 

Satan fought apostles Paul, Peter, John, and other early church leaders then and he is still fighting the saints today. The testimony of the scriptures and history of the church Martyrs is that they prevailed through grace. We cannot do with a lesser assistance. We all need grace to remain in the kingdom after we have first entered in. 

3. The benefits of grace for the surely saved souls
There are many souls out there that say they are saved "by the grace of God." Titus 3&#58;11-14 questions such claims.

For the surely saved souls, grace is the instrument through which saints have access to the great and precious promises pertaining to life and godliness prepared for them (2 Peter 1&#58;3-4). We will mention but a few here.

i.  Freedom from the power of Satan that brings bondage through the storms of fear (Judges 5&#58;12-13;  4&#58;8,6-7) sicknesses (Isaiah 53&#58;5), devastating flood of multiple attacks (Isaiah 59&#58;19), etc.

ii. Assurance of returning back to the paradise of God. Privileges lost in Genesis 2&#58;9 when God drove man away because of sin (Genesis 3&#58;23-24) regained through grace in Revelations 2&#58;7; 22&#58;2, 14.

iii. Assurance of answers to prayers (Psalms 50&#58;15,16; Isaiah 65&#58;24)

iv. Prosperity in health and material things (3 John 1&#58;2)

The Holy Scriptures describes God's promises for those that escape the corruption that is in the word as "great and precious". These promises cover everything that pertains unto life (here on earth) and godliness (by which we will enter heaven). We should therefore strive to escape sin's corruption in order to inherit these promises. The Lord will do this for every willing & determined soul in Jesus name.
