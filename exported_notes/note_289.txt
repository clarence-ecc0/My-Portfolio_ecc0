{{text:STS&#58; Lesson 125&#58; Forgiveness, faith and gratitude enjoined.

There will be offences CERTAINLY even among brethren. Christ said this and we are commanded to forgive no matter what, if the offender has repented of his offences (if he refuses to repent, there is a deliberate resolution plan given by Christ&#58; talk to a brother yourself and if he persists, take one or two more in addition to yourself &#123;so that there are now two or three witnesses&#125;, matured Christians and if he resists them also, take the matter to the church and if he persists still, the church will declare him as a heathen. Take note that Christ said, whatsoever the church shall bind on earth &#123;a man declared as a heathen&#125; is bound/upheld in heaven). To forgive every offence no matter what is a difficult thing that can only be accomplished through grace. If we do this, it marks two things&#58; our love for our brethren and a willingness to obey the Lord. The act is composed of three characters&#58; faith, forgiveness and gratitude. 
 If a man will want to keep offending because he will be forgiven, he must understand that there is woe pronounced on one who offends ceaselessly. See Mathew 18&#58;7; Luke 17&#58;1


TOPIC&#58; FORGIVENESS IN THE FELLOWSHIP OF THE FATHER'S FAMILY

Mathew 18&#58;15-35

Point-1&#58; The forgiveness of personal offences among the brethren 

Mathew 18&#58;15-17; 18&#58;3; 

a. The indispensable forgiveness at New birth
Mathew 18&#58;3; Acts 5&#58;30-31; Colossians 1&#58;13-14
The forgiveness the Bible is talking about is among brethren in the family. The fact that I have forgiven my offender does not mean that Gid has forgiven him. Forgiven offenders also need the forgiveness of God to enter the Kingdom of Christ. When crises is common in a family, it tells us that probably they (one or both) are not born again.

b. The initiated forgiveness of offending brother
Mathew 18&#58;15; Luke 17&#58;3-4&#58; Colossians 3&#58;12-17
A woman leader was offended by a choir master. The Spirit revealed to her to seek the younger choir master and reconcile because she nursed bitterness against him. She refused to initiate the reconciliation process. She died and was revealed, she went to hell. She didn't go to hell because of the offence she suffered but because she harboured bitterness.

c. The insufficient forgiveness of obstinate backsliders
Mathew 18&#58;17; Hosea 11&#58;7,12; Jeremiah 8&#58;4-9,20-22
We must forgive obstinate backsliders who refuse to repent after all things are done so that they do not drag you to their level.
As tough as the terrorism of king Saul was against David, David forgave him. But David's forgiveness of David will not take Saul to heaven. 

Point-2&#58;  The fullness of powerful overcomers in all battles 

a. The binding power of an unfettered believer
Mathew 18&#58;18; 16&#58;19
A believer only has power to bind and unbind if he himself is unfettered from sin. 
Two young men came together to start a ministry and God blessed the church and it grew. Next barrier of Satan was who was to be leader. There was no agreement until church elders decided one of them must be the leader. This was grudgingly accepted but bitterness reigned. Both died and did not make it to heaven despite all their work.

b. The boundless possibilities of United brethren
Mathew 18&#58;19-20
When brethren are united, Satan cannot stand against them. He will therefore and actually doing all he can to disunite the brethren. God doesn't work with divided people. Family blessings are not released when husband wife are not in unity. 


c. The benevolent pilgrim with unlimited brotherliness
Mathew 18&#58;21-22
There is no one that will offend you seventy times seven in a day. What this means is that we must forgive all, always. This is possible only through genuine salvation. Our hearts and thoughts towards offenders must be void of bitterness, wrath, etc. It should be thoughts of love, peace, accommodation.

Point-3&#58;  The forfeiture of precious opportunities through bitterness 

Mathew 18&#58;23-35

a. The fate of unmerciful oppressor under tormenting
Mathew 18&#58;32, 34. 
The initial forgiveness that the servant debtor won was lost because he would not forgive a fellow servant. He lost all that precious opportunity. In our daily engagements, we must not be cruel to our debtors, staff or those under us in office. Show mercy to receive mercy (Mathew 5&#58;7)


b. The future of unrepentant offenders in torment 
Mathew 18&#58;7-9
If you are very serious in going to heaven, make provisions for offences (disappointments) from brethren. This is so, so that man's disappointment will not cause you to backslide and miss heaven. Those that love to lead others to temptations by provoking their Spirit through unpaid debt, they should know that great torment awaits them in eternity. 

c. The fullness of unwavering obedience and teachableness 
Ephesians 3&#58;19-21
