{{text:TOPIC&#58; RAHAB FULLY PERSUADED, NOT FATALLY PASSIVE, IN FAITH 

James 2&#58;25-26; Hebrews 11&#58;31
The people that are active in faith will take steps to ensure they are saved. Those that are passive will say, well, those that will be saved, will be saved; they will not take any active step. They are not deliberate, decisive on the matter of seeing the King in His glory. 

Point-1&#58; Experiencing conversion and personal confirmation through regenerating faith

James 2&#58;25
Remember, Abraham never said to the Lord when He called him, if you want to save me, save me. He took active step to be saved and blessed. Similar to what Rahab did. She seized upon the opportunity for salvation and she was saved.

a. Rahab conviction from perceived facts
We should build our convictions from the facts we already have in the Bible.
Joshua 2&#58;9-11; Mathew 13&#58;14-15; No John 3&#58;1-3
Rahab analysed what she heard of the God of Israel and the fear in her own country and was convinced (even before Israel's army crossed Jordan or marched around her city for 7 days) that God had given Israel their land. Rahab said, "for the Lord your God, He is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath". Many people have heard more than Rahab the harlot heard; why have their convictions not been built upon what they have heard and by them, believe in the Lord and be saved? The Pharisees heard so much from Christ Himself and yet were not saved!

b. Rahab's confessions with personal faith
Repentance and regeneration come out of personal decision.
Pharaoh said I have sinned and yet he suffered afterward; King Saul said the same thing and because his confession was not towards forsaking the confessed sin, he was not delivered. After Rahab's confession, she pleaded that she and her household would be spared. God did not destroy the amorites unjustly (Moses made this clear in Deuteronomy chapters 9 & 10) but because of their great sins. Rahab was an amorites as well and she was spared. She repented and through marriage, became a great grandparent of Christ. Many that declare "I believe!, I believe!", have they turned around from their old sins like Rahab? Rahab didn't continue as a harlot in Israel. 

Romans 10&#58;9-10; Joshua 2&#58;9,12
Salvation is not just by the habitual mouth confession; it must be associated with a deep belief in the heart that God is God and must be obeyed.


c. Rahab's commitment in persevering faithfulness 
Joshua 2&#58;17-20; 6&#58;22-23; Acts 13&#58;43; 14&#58;22
Faithfulness to the Lord in the faith we declared at the beginning of our salvation is necessary for sustained salvation. After understanding the righteous laws of God in the land of Israel, Rahab did not go back to her immoral ways but became a housewife of renown even into this day. She persevered in her new-found faith in the God of Israel.

Point-2&#58; Escaping condemnation for past corruption through recognized faith

a. Repentant harlot saved and justified by faith
Mathew 21&#58;31-31; 2 Peter 3&#58;9
Christ was very bold, to tell the Pharisees that the harlots and the publicans were going to heaven before them (preferred to them). There is no salvation without repentance, even joining the Deeper Life (without true repentance evidenced by faith) cannot save. 

b. Recovered humans, separated by justification from filthiness 
2 Corinthians 7&#58;1,10; James 1&#58;21,27; Proverbs 30&#58;2
We ought to lay apart all filthiness before death comes. Take note that "where the tree falleth, there it remaineth. By the same token, a man's state at death is his final state (Eccl 11&#58;3). It can't be changed by prayers at the funeral.

c. The reprobate hypocrite who will suffer judgment for falsehood 
James 2&#58;20,26; Titus 1&#58;16
Those that by declarations say they know God but their works (actions) declare the opposite are reprobates. If hypocrites and liars and other sinners are allowed to enter heaven, heaven will not be different from the present evil, wicked world. What then could be the justification for any man to deny himself the pleasant things just to go to a place similar to the current evil world? This doesn't make any sense.


Point-3&#58; Enduring consecration and permanent characteristics of resilient faith

Hebrews 11&#58;31

a. The expectant faith despite the condemnation of the people
Joshua 2&#58;8-10; Hebrews 11&#58;8
Whatever your path must have been before you met the Lord, that path must surely change in order to see God. We need to look at our past lives

b. The expected faithfulness and dependence on the condition of promise.


Joshua 2&#58;14-15, 17-19
Rahab and all her relations were under the cover of the scarlet curtain by the window. As many of Rahab's relations that dwelt in the room under the scarlet protection, they were protected. But if anyone moves out of the cover, his death is upon his head. Isn't that similar to the covering by the death of Christ (the scarlet colored blood of Christ)?
