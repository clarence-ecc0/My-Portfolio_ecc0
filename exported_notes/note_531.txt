The association with Ahab and his family
Although he walked after the Lord and not after idolatrous Baal, He made peace with Ahab, the king of Israel. 
1 Kings 22&#58;2-4

This peace package included marriages between his family and the Ahab's family, going on joint military expedition, and joint business partnership, as we will see later.


The profile of the idolatrous household of Ahab

In 1 Kings 18&#58;4, we learnt that Jezebel (Ahab's wife) cut off (destroyed) the prophets of the LORD; this is like saying the family you as a child of God is fellowshipping was responsible for the destruction of God's prophets.

 In 1 Kings 18&#58;19, we also learnt that King Ahab was commanded to gather to Elijah all Israel unto mount Carmel, and the prophets of Baal four hundred and fifty, and the prophets of the groves four hundred, which eat at Jezebel's table.


 In 1 Kings 21&#58;25, we learnt again that there was none like unto Ahab, which did sell himself to work wickedness in the sight of the LORD, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up.

King Jehoshaphat's notable weakness 
A common refrain of King Jehoshaphat was,  "I am as thou art, my people as thy people" (1 Kings 22&#58;4; 2 Kings 3&#58;7)". How can this be?

Military expedition&#58;
✓ He said this to Ahab when he wanted to fight the Syrian army at Ramoth-gilead (1 Kings 22&#58;4); 
✓ after the death of Ahab, he said it again to his son (King Jehoram) when he wanted to fight the king of Moab (2 Kings 3&#58;7). 
On both occasions, God has to intervene to deliver Jehoshaphat.

Business partnership&#58; Again, Jehoshaphat joined himself to King Ahaziah (Ahab's son), to build ships at Tarshish to go to Eziongeber, to prospect for gold but the Lord broke his ship. (2 Chronicles 20&#58;35-37)
